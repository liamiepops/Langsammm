Three directions for the on-page panel

The panel currently lists six sliders with no indication of what any of them are doing to the sound. Each direction below solves that differently. Throughout, amber is always the warped signal and cyan is always the original, so the colour carries the idea rather than just decorating it. The plot in direction A is drawn from a real frame of demo-material.wav, not a sketch.

Direction A

Scope

For when you want to see the algorithm working.

The gain curve is the whole effect in one picture: everything Slowform does is a per-bin gain, so plotting it leaves nothing hidden. The two envelopes are drawn over each other and the amber fill between them is literally what you are hearing.

  • Depth and wobble as live numbers turn the envelope-res tradeoff into something you watch instead of guess at. Depth stops rising, wobble keeps climbing, and you can see the moment it stops being worth it.
  • Ceiling and delay sit in the same strip, so you always know whether the limiter is colouring what you are judging.
  • Tooltips carry the measured numbers rather than generic descriptions.
Build cost: highest. Needs a new wasm export to snapshot the envelope, warped envelope and gain into a buffer, posted to the panel at about 15 Hz. Roughly 50 lines of Rust, a message in the worklet, and a canvas draw. Depth and wobble are cheap running statistics over the gain curve.

Direction B

Chain

For making it obvious what happens, and in what order.

Laid out as the actual signal path rather than a flat list, so each control sits inside the stage it belongs to. The numbering is real here: audio genuinely passes through these three stages in sequence, and the crossover only makes sense as something that happens inside stage two.

  • The ruler in stage one shows the two grids side by side, which explains the interval faster than 0.8409 ever will.
  • Controls are renamed to what they do rather than how they work. "Protect bass below" and "ease off on hits" need no tooltip at all.
  • Stage three finally makes the loudness matcher and the ceiling visible, so you can see when something other than the warp is touching the sound.
  • The idle dot on stage three tells you nothing is being corrected right now, which is the state you want during a listening test.
Build cost: lowest of the three. No new DSP work. Every value shown is already sent to the panel today, including the limiter gain. This is a reorganisation plus two small SVGs.

Direction C

Listening

For judging the sound rather than operating the tool.

The two-state bar is the hero, because the question you kept hitting was not which value a slider is on. It was which of the two things you are currently hearing. That state is now the largest element on the panel, and it lights up as you hold the key.

  • Everything except the interval and one strength figure is collapsed behind tune. You open it deliberately.
  • The tooltip states the thing that is easy to get wrong: both sides are slowed, so the comparison is the warp alone.
  • Fits the existing 296px, and stops competing with the video.
Build cost: low. Mostly a reorganisation of what exists. The one new value is "effect strength", which is the same running statistic direction A calls depth.

Where I would land

B for the structure, with A's readout strip dropped into stage three. B fixes the real problem, which is that a flat list of six sliders gives no clue that three of them only make sense inside one stage of the chain, and it costs nothing to build. The one genuinely new thing worth paying for is depth and wobble as live numbers, because that pair turns the envelope-res question from a matter of taste into something you can read off. The full gain plot from A is the most fun and the least necessary; it is worth doing once B is in and you know what you actually look at.
